I am an Educational Policy and Leadership Ph.D. candidate at Vanderbilt Peabody College where I focus on the intersection of education and student health. I use quantitative methods to study the intersection of education and student health with the intention of informing policy towards improved mental and physical health and academic outcomes. In particular, my current work examines the influences of school-based health centers (SBHCs), the impacts of technology, and long-run trends in student educational outcomes.

I have an undergraduate degree from the University of Virginia where I double majored in economics and statistics with a concentration in econometrics, minoring in philosophy. Throughout my undergraduate career, I pursued research in development economics and economics of education.

My dissertation uses Tennessee state educational and Medicaid data and quasi-experimental methods to examine variation in individualized student healthcare utilization as well as assess the impacts of federal grants aimed at establishing and expanding SBHCs. Additionally, I examine long-run trends in Michigan state student absenteeism through multi-level modeling analysis. Collectively, I am dedicated to examining and advocating for comprehensive efforts towards systematic change focused on ensuring student, presence, engagement, and health at school through methodically rigorous and theoretically grounded research aimed at all levels of policy.
